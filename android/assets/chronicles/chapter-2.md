### Page One

The crown's sundered halves did not rest upon the stone. They listened.

The traveler felt that truth before they touched it. Air pressed in, thick with old oaths. The bandit clerk muttered a prayer he'd never learned. Down the staircase, darkness was not empty. It was crowded with waiting.

The voice did not speak again, but the crown warmed as if recognizing blood. Above, Ravenshollow drank and laughed, unaware that its foundations had begun to choose sides.

### Page Two

In Thornhaven, Captain Edric Voss ordered extra watch fires, though his soldiers swore their tinder hissed at the blue flame, as if offended. Merchants demanded escorts. Caravans would not leave at dawn.

In the archives, Lady Seraphine compared the vanished manuscript to older copies. The absence moved. It was not a missing page but a wound that resisted ink. She found a margin note in a different hand: “When remembrance returns, it will not ask permission.”

### Page Three

In the mountains above Vaeloria, the first spire shuddered as if waking from a long fever. Shepherds fled when its surface lit with crawling script. Children dreamed of towers speaking their names. One child answered in their sleep, and a distant tower answered back.

The spire did not reveal land yet. It revealed a grave, unnamed, beneath ice.

### Page Four

In Ravenshollow, the traveler lifted one half of the crown. Pain flashed behind their eyes, not theirs alone. They saw a hall of seven thrones, six gleaming, one shattered. A king knelt voluntarily, and chains were offered like a gift.

The traveler dropped the crown, gasping. The clerk caught it, and for a heartbeat his pupils turned silver.

“It knows us,” he whispered. “Not who we are, who we'll be.”

### Page Five

Seraphine rode before dawn, ignoring Aldric's protests. The road to the spire felt shorter than maps allowed. Reality bent toward remembrance. At the hill, her horse refused another step. She climbed alone.

The spire's surface formed a single question without words. It was her childhood regret given shape. She answered aloud, voice shaking.

“I left because I was afraid.”

The spire flared, and the map of the valley burned itself into her mind. Along with it, a warning: “Truth restored demands truth paid.”

### Page Six

In the dark beneath Ravenshollow, the second spire stirred in sympathy. Its light was not blue or silver, but bruised violet. The bandit clerk screamed as memories flooded in—roads he never walked, betrayals he never committed, a promise he somehow broke before birth.

The traveler dragged him back, both coughing blood onto ancient steps. Above them, Ravenshollow's tavern signs swung without wind, and every dog in town began to howl at once.

### Page Seven

Far away, Aldric felt the crown's echo and finally understood the voice had never been offering peace. It offered an ending. He ordered the banners taken down so the gods could not see them.

In the same hour, a child in a mountain hut drew seven crowns in ash and erased one before anyone taught them numbers.

The world was remembering without consent.
