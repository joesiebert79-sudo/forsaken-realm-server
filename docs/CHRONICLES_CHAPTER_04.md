# Forsaken Realm Chronicles
## Book One — The Age of Splendor
### Chapter Four — The Bell That Rang Beneath the Earth

The bells of Ravenshollow had not rung in forty-three years.

That was what the oldest inhabitants insisted, though none could agree upon the reason. Some claimed the bell tower had been abandoned after a plague. Others spoke of a fire, a murdered priest, or a winter so terrible that the townspeople had melted the bells to make weapons.

Yet on this night, every bell in Ravenshollow was ringing.

And the tower that held them was empty.

The traveler stood in the muddy street, one hand still gripping the bandit clerk's collar. Behind them, the hidden stairway descended into darkness. Before them, tavern doors hung open, merchants abandoned their stalls, and frightened townspeople gathered beneath a moon obscured by gathering clouds.

The bells continued.

Their sound was wrong.

Each toll seemed to come from somewhere beneath the earth, rising through the cobblestones before reaching the air. With every strike, the ground trembled faintly beneath their feet.

The clerk stared toward the old tower.

“That's impossible,” he whispered.

The traveler released him.

“You've said that three times tonight.”

“And every time, something worse has happened.”

A woman emerged from a nearby house carrying a sleeping child. The little girl's eyes were closed, but her lips moved steadily.

The traveler stepped closer.

“What is she saying?”

The woman shook her head.

“I don't know. She won't wake.”

The traveler leaned down.

The child's voice was barely audible.

“Edran. Malvek. Sorath. Caldris.”

Four names.

Then a fifth.

Then a sixth.

Before she could speak the seventh, the bells stopped.

The silence that followed was so sudden that several townspeople cried out.

The little girl's eyes opened.

She looked directly at the traveler.

“There's someone under the mountain,” she said.

Then she began to weep.

Far to the north, beneath the vaulted ceilings of Vaeloria's royal archives, Lady Seraphine was discovering that history itself had begun to unravel.

She had spent the night surrounded by manuscripts, royal genealogies, and ancient treaties. Every record concerning the Seven Great Kingdoms had changed.

Names that had once been written clearly were now smudged beyond recognition. Dates contradicted one another. Entire generations had vanished from the chronicles.

But one document remained untouched.

It was a treaty older than Vaeloria itself, sealed with seven impressions of a crown.

Six seals were broken.

The seventh remained whole.

Seraphine held the parchment toward the candlelight.

Beneath the final seal, hidden within the fibers of the ancient paper, a sentence slowly appeared.

*The faithful king was not buried to punish his betrayal.*

She leaned closer.

More words emerged.

*He was buried because he refused to betray us.*

Seraphine lowered the parchment.

For years, the kingdom's historians had taught that the ancient rulers imprisoned a terrible evil beneath Hollow Crown. Their victory had preserved the world and ushered in the Age of Splendor.

But the treaty suggested something else.

Someone had been sacrificed.

Not an enemy.

An ally.

And the six surviving kings had erased his name.

The archive doors opened.

King Aldric entered without his guards.

He looked older than he had that morning.

“Tell me what you've found,” he said.

Seraphine hesitated.

“Your Majesty, I believe the histories are lying to us.”

Aldric approached the table.

“Histories do not lie, Lady Seraphine. Men do.”

She turned the parchment toward him.

The king read the newly revealed words.

For a long moment, he said nothing.

Then he reached beneath his cloak and withdrew a small silver object.

It was a ring bearing the image of a crown divided in two.

Seraphine stared.

“Where did you get that?”

“My father gave it to me before he died.”

“Did he tell you what it meant?”

Aldric's expression hardened.

“He told me never to wear it.”

The candle flames bent toward the ring.

And somewhere beneath the palace, a bell began to toll.

In Ravenshollow, the traveler had returned to the old bell tower.

The clerk followed reluctantly, clutching a rusted dagger he had taken from a fallen guard.

The tower's wooden doors were sealed with iron chains.

But the chains had not been broken.

They had been opened.

Each link lay neatly upon the ground, arranged in a perfect circle.

The traveler pushed the doors inward.

Inside, dust covered the floor. Broken stairs climbed toward an empty chamber where the bells had once hung.

At the center of the tower stood a stone pedestal.

Upon it rested a book.

Its cover was black leather, cracked with age, and bound by seven narrow bands of tarnished silver.

The traveler approached.

The clerk caught their arm.

“Don't.”

The traveler looked at him.

“Another rule?”

The clerk nodded.

“My grandfather said there were books beneath Ravenshollow that should never be opened.”

“And what happened to the people who opened them?”

The clerk's voice trembled.

“They remembered things they hadn't lived.”

The traveler turned back toward the pedestal.

Across the book's cover, letters began to emerge.

They formed a single sentence.

*What was forgotten shall be witnessed.*

The traveler placed a hand upon the cover.

The silver bands released themselves.

One by one, they fell away.

The book opened.

And the traveler saw a kingdom burning.

Not Ravenshollow.

Not Vaeloria.

A city of white towers and golden bridges, surrounded by fields of flowering trees. Thousands of people crowded its streets beneath banners bearing the symbol of a silver crown.

At the center stood a palace with seven great doors.

Six were open.

The seventh was sealed.

Before that final door knelt a king dressed in plain white robes.

His crown lay upon the ground beside him.

Around him stood six other rulers, their faces hidden beneath ceremonial veils.

One held a sword.

Another held a chain.

A third carried a bowl filled with black earth.

The kneeling king raised his head.

“You cannot imprison what you have invited,” he told them.

One of the veiled rulers stepped forward.

“We have no choice.”

“You had a choice when the whispers began.”

The ruler lowered his gaze.

The kneeling king continued.

“You listened because it promised you power. You opened the mountain because it promised you eternity. And now you would bury me beneath the consequences of your hunger.”

The six rulers remained silent.

Then the king in white stood.

“If my imprisonment will spare the innocent, I will enter willingly.”

He looked toward the sealed door.

“But understand this.”

The vision trembled.

“Stone cannot hold a lie forever.”

The traveler stumbled backward.

The book slammed shut.

The tower vanished from sight for a heartbeat, replaced by darkness and the distant sound of chains dragging across stone.

Then Ravenshollow returned.

The clerk was shouting.

The traveler could barely hear him.

Because another voice had begun speaking inside their mind.

It was neither cruel nor kind.

It sounded exhausted.

*Find the names.*

The traveler gripped the pedestal.

*Find the six who broke their oath.*

The voice weakened.

*And remember the seventh.*

Outside, the bells began ringing again.

But this time, they were not alone.

Across the Seven Great Kingdoms, ancient towers answered.

In Thornhaven, Captain Edric Voss watched a column of blue fire rise from the nearest Spire of Remembrance. Its light stretched into the clouds, illuminating roads and ruins that had remained hidden for generations.

In the northern mountains, shepherds fell to their knees as forgotten symbols blazed across the snow.

In the royal archives, Seraphine watched the seventh seal split open.

And beneath Hollow Crown, far below the deepest mines ever carved by mortal hands, something opened its eyes.

For centuries, it had listened to the footsteps of kings.

It had heard empires rise and crumble.

It had endured the silence of its prison.

Now, for the first time in an age, it smiled.

Because the world had begun to remember.

And remembrance was the first crack in the door.
