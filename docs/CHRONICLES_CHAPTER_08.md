# Forsaken Realm Chronicles
## Book One — The Age of Splendor
### Chapter Eight — The Keeper of Ash

*The seventh grave had begun to knock.*

Rain fell over the burial ground in thin, silver threads, but not a drop touched the seven stones. The traveler stood between them, listening as three slow knocks sounded again from beneath the earth.

The bandit clerk backed away. “Tell me that's someone trapped down there.”

“Someone is,” the traveler answered.

“Then we dig.”

The traveler knelt beside the seventh stone. Its surface was smooth where a name should have been, yet the earth around it had been disturbed recently. Not by a shovel. The soil had risen from below, as though something buried had tried to push its way into the world.

Old Brenn emerged from the rain carrying a lantern and an iron spade. He had lived beside the cemetery for forty-three years, and in all that time the villagers had considered him a harmless old fool.

Tonight, he looked like a man who had finally run out of places to hide.

“Don't break the ground,” he said.

The clerk turned. “There's a child under there!”

“No.” Brenn's voice trembled. “There's a memory wearing a child's voice.”

The knocking stopped.

Then the seventh grave spoke.

“Brenn. You promised you would remember.”

The old man closed his eyes.

“I know,” he whispered. “And I have.”

---

The traveler had learned that fear made people lie, but guilt made them tell the truth in fragments.

Brenn led them to a cottage beyond the cemetery, where the windows were shuttered and the hearth had been cold for years. He lit seven candles on the table. Six burned yellow. The seventh turned blue.

“Who are you?” the traveler asked.

“A gravedigger.”

“Gravediggers don't know how to keep voices beneath the earth.”

Brenn stared into the blue flame. “Once, we were called Keepers of Ash.”

The clerk leaned forward.

Brenn explained that after the Seven Kingdoms made their terrible bargain beneath Hollow Crown, the six surviving kings ordered every account of the seventh king destroyed. His portraits were burned. His monuments were broken. His name was cut from stone, scraped from parchment, and forbidden in royal courts.

But the kings discovered that history could not be destroyed so easily.

Whenever a record burned, its ashes retained an echo. Sometimes a word. Sometimes a face. Sometimes the final breath of the person who had written it.

So the kings created the Keepers of Ash: servants sworn to collect those remnants, seal them beneath seven graves, and prevent the truth from returning.

“And you were one of them?” the clerk asked.

Brenn shook his head.

“My father was. His father before him. The duty passed down through generations. Each keeper inherited the ashes and the silence.”

“Why keep them at all?” asked the traveler. “Why not scatter them?”

“Because the six kings were afraid of forgetting the very thing they had done. They wanted the truth hidden from their people, but preserved for themselves.”

He reached beneath the floorboards and drew out a narrow wooden box, blackened as if it had survived a great fire.

Inside lay a handful of gray ash and a small silver key.

“Seven graves,” Brenn said. “Seven witnesses. But only six were meant to remain silent.”

“And the seventh?”

Brenn looked toward the cemetery.

“The seventh was never a witness.”

He swallowed.

“It was a door.”

---

Far north, beneath the abandoned chapel of Saint Orwyn, King Aldric held the tarnished silver crown in both hands.

The ancient chamber around him had grown warmer. Beneath the seventh throne, a heartbeat sounded through the stone, steady and painfully human.

Lady Seraphine examined the inscriptions circling the floor. Most were written in the old royal tongue, but beneath them lay another script, carved so deeply that even centuries of deliberate damage had failed to erase it.

“Your Majesty,” she said, “the six kings didn't merely bury the truth. They divided it.”

Aldric looked up.

“Each kingdom received a portion of the original oath. No one ruler could possess the whole account. The pieces were hidden in places their descendants would never think to search.”

“The spires?”

“Some of them. Others were entrusted to people.”

Aldric remembered the vision of the seventh king standing before the forbidden gate, offering himself to hold back the darkness while the six drew their swords behind him.

“Why would the six preserve evidence of their betrayal?”

“Because they needed it,” Seraphine answered. “The oath wasn't only a confession. It was part of the binding.”

The chamber trembled.

A line of blue light traveled across the seventh throne. Then a voice spoke, faint but unmistakable.

“The chain is made of remembrance.”

Aldric stood motionless.

“Who speaks?”

“The one you have forgotten.”

The king stepped closer.

“Tell me your name.”

For a moment, the chamber fell silent.

Then the voice replied:

“I cannot give what your fathers took from me.”

A crack spread across the stone floor.

Seraphine seized Aldric's arm. “We need to leave.”

But the voice spoke again.

“Find the Keeper of Ash. Before the seventh grave opens.”

---

At Thornhaven, Captain Edric Voss rode through the eastern gate with twelve soldiers and a map that had not existed the previous morning.

The parchment was old, but the ink upon it was new. Roads appeared whenever moonlight touched the page, winding through forests and mountains that no living cartographer had surveyed.

Garran rode beside him.

“You believe the spire showed you the truth?”

“I believe it showed me something someone worked very hard to conceal.”

“And if it's a trap?”

“Then whoever laid it knows we're coming.”

They reached the remains of an ancient watchtower before dawn. The tower stood beside a narrow road buried beneath centuries of moss. Seven iron bells hung inside its ruined archway.

Six were cracked.

The seventh had no clapper.

Edric dismounted and examined the stonework. Someone had carved a sentence into the lowest step.

*When the bells are silent, listen for the ashes.*

Garran brushed away the moss.

“Captain, there's something underneath.”

A second inscription appeared, its letters glowing faintly beneath his fingers.

*The dead remember what the living surrender.*

Before Edric could speak, the seventh bell rang.

There was no sound in the air. Instead, he felt the note inside his chest, as though someone had struck a hammer against his heart.

His soldiers staggered. Horses reared. The map in his hand burst into blue fire.

Yet the parchment did not burn.

Across its surface, a single location appeared: a cemetery south of Ravenshollow.

Edric stared at the name written beneath it.

**The Keeper's Rest.**

“Change of course,” he ordered.

Garran looked toward the northern mountains. “But Hollow Crown—”

“Can wait. Whatever holds that mountain closed is failing. And someone at that cemetery may know why.”

---

In Brenn's cottage, the silver key had begun to turn by itself.

The traveler watched it rotate inside the wooden box, scraping a circle through the ash.

“What's it opening?” the clerk asked.

Brenn did not answer.

Outside, the rain stopped. The silence that followed was worse than the storm.

Then footsteps approached the cottage.

One pair.

Slow. Uneven.

The traveler drew a blade and moved toward the door.

“Don't,” Brenn whispered.

The footsteps stopped.

Three knocks sounded against the wood.

The clerk stared at the old man. “That's the same knocking.”

Brenn nodded.

A child's voice came from outside.

“Keeper, the ashes are cold.”

Brenn covered his face.

“Keeper, the door is lonely.”

The traveler gripped the latch.

“Don't open it,” Brenn pleaded. “It wants someone to invite it inside.”

The voice changed.

It became that of a woman, gentle and exhausted.

“Father?”

Brenn's face crumpled.

The traveler saw the truth then. Whatever stood outside had searched the old man's memories and found the one voice he could not bear to refuse.

“My daughter died in the winter fever,” Brenn said. “She was nine.”

The clerk stepped between him and the door.

“Then that isn't her.”

“I know.”

But Brenn was already reaching for the latch.

The traveler caught his wrist.

“Your daughter wouldn't ask you to betray the living.”

For one long moment, Brenn struggled.

Then his hand fell.

Outside, the woman's voice became something vast and hollow.

“Six kingdoms gave me their dead. One kingdom gave me its king. And still you deny me entry.”

The cottage shook.

Ash rose from the wooden box, forming a narrow spiral above the table. Within it appeared a vision of the hall beneath Hollow Crown.

Six kings stood before the forbidden gate.

The seventh faced them, his silver crown held against his chest.

“You have heard its promise,” he told them. “But you have not heard its price.”

One of the six stepped forward.

“It offers us life beyond death.”

“No,” the seventh answered. “It offers to wear our lives after death.”

The vision changed.

The six kings drew their swords, but they did not strike the seventh.

Instead, they cut their own palms and pressed bloody hands against the gate.

The traveler understood.

The betrayal had begun before the seventh king was buried.

The six had willingly invited the darkness into the world.

And the seventh had been forced to remain behind to contain what they had released.

The ash collapsed.

Outside, something screamed without breath.

Brenn seized the silver key.

“Now you understand why the truth was hidden. The six were not deceived. They knew.”

The traveler looked toward the cemetery.

“And the seventh grave?”

“It holds the final words of their oath.”

“Then we need to open it.”

Brenn's eyes filled with terror.

“If you open that grave, the darkness will know where the last words are kept.”

“It already knows,” the traveler said. “It's standing outside your door.”

---

The cemetery had changed.

The six outer stones now faced inward, their carved crowns illuminated by pale blue fire. The seventh stood in the center, its blank surface split by a vertical seam.

Brenn approached with the key.

“The Keepers were taught that remembering would destroy the realm,” he said. “My father believed it. His father believed it. I believed it until I heard my daughter calling from the grave.”

“What changed?” asked the clerk.

“I remembered that she was afraid of the dark. She would never have asked me to open a door into it.”

He placed the silver key into the seam.

Stone unfolded.

Beneath the grave lay no coffin, no bones, and no buried child.

Only a book.

Its cover was made of blackened leather, its pages edged with silver. Seven chains bound it shut. Six were broken. The seventh remained whole.

The traveler lifted it carefully.

Upon the cover, words appeared in glowing ash.

*The Oath of the Seven.*

The clerk touched the remaining chain.

A vision flashed before them: a young king, kneeling alone beneath Hollow Crown, pressing his crown against a door of black iron.

He was bleeding.

He was afraid.

But he did not retreat.

A voice spoke from the book.

“Let the living remember.”

The seventh chain fell away.

The first page opened.

There, written in the hand of the forgotten king, was a single sentence:

*I did not bind the darkness with my life. I bound it with the promise that the world would one day know the truth.*

The traveler read it aloud.

Far beneath Hollow Crown, the seventh chain blazed silver.

The darkness recoiled.

For the first time in centuries, the forgotten king drew a full breath.

And across the realm, every awakened Spire of Remembrance answered with a brilliant pillar of light.

The clerk looked toward the distant mountains.

“Did we save him?”

The traveler turned the page.

More writing appeared, slowly, as though an unseen hand were remembering how to form the letters.

“No,” the traveler said.

“Then what have we done?”

The traveler read the next sentence, and felt the blood turn cold.

*When the truth is restored, the six must answer for their bargain.*

In the darkness beyond the forbidden gate, six ancient voices began to laugh.

The traveler closed the book.

“We've given him a chance.”

Far away, in the chamber beneath Saint Orwyn's chapel, Aldric heard the laughter too.

And for the first time, he recognized one of the voices.

It belonged to his father.

**End of Chapter Eight — The Keeper of Ash.**
