# Forsaken Realm Chronicles
## Book One — The Age of Splendor
### Chapter Six — The Debt of Seven Kings

Rain had been falling over Vaeloria for nine days.

It gathered in the ruts of forgotten roads, spilled from the mouths of stone gargoyles, and seeped into graves whose occupants had been dead longer than the kingdom itself. Farmers watched their fields drown beneath a gray sky. Shepherds reported strange lights moving through the northern hills. At night, the wolves no longer howled. They gathered in circles and stared toward Hollow Crown.

In the abandoned chapel of Saint Orwyn, King Aldric stood before an altar that had not witnessed a prayer in more than two centuries.

Seven candles burned upon its surface. He had lit none of them. Six gave off the warm golden light of ordinary flame. The seventh burned a cold, unwavering blue, casting shadows that stretched across the walls in directions no fire should have permitted.

Aldric had arrived before sunset, accompanied by four royal guards. He had ordered them to wait beyond the chapel gates. That had been three hours ago. He had not heard a sound from them since.

His father had warned him about blue fire. Not in the language of kings, with its careful phrases and veiled threats, but in the frightened whisper of a dying man: *When the old flames turn blue, my son, remember that a crown is not always a gift.*

Aldric had been seventeen when those words were spoken. He had spent the following twenty-eight years trying to forget them.

Footsteps sounded beyond the chapel doors. Lady Seraphine entered beneath the broken archway, her cloak dark with rain. Mud clung to the hem of her traveling dress, and a leather satchel hung heavily from one shoulder.

“You came,” Aldric said.

“You sent for me.”

“I sent no one.”

Seraphine withdrew a folded parchment. “The messenger carried your seal.”

The royal crest was perfect. Even the tiny fracture running through the left wing of the crowned falcon had been reproduced. Only three people in Vaeloria possessed a seal capable of making that impression. One was Aldric himself. One was the Lord Chancellor. The third had been buried beneath the palace chapel eleven years earlier.

Inside, written in a hand he recognized but could not place, were seven words: *The debt is due. Bring the witness.*

“Tell me what you've discovered,” he said.

Seraphine set the ancient treaty upon the altar. Six seals were broken. The seventh, whole when she had found it, now bore a hairline fracture.

“The histories of the Seven Great Kingdoms are wrong. They tell us the ancient kings united to imprison a great evil beneath Hollow Crown. But the seventh king did not betray the others. He refused to join whatever bargain they made.”

“And the six?”

“They buried him.”

“Alive?”

“I believe so. The treaty doesn't describe his imprisonment as punishment. It describes it as payment.”

“Payment for what?”

“I don't know.”

The seventh candle flickered. A low scraping sound came from beneath the altar. Stone dragged against stone, and a voice spoke from beneath their feet.

“Six kings took what was never theirs. Six kings swore an oath they never intended to keep. And the seventh paid their debt.”

The altar cracked. Aldric remembered his father's deathbed warning: *When the seventh remembers, the blood of the six shall answer.*

The stone split apart, revealing a staircase descending into absolute darkness. From below came the measured sound of footsteps. Someone was climbing toward them.

---

In Ravenshollow, morning arrived without birdsong. Merchants spoke in whispers, mothers kept children indoors, and hired swords gathered near the gates.

The traveler sat above a tavern, drawing seven crowns on parchment. Six unbroken. One divided.

“You're drawing it wrong,” said the bandit clerk from the window. “The seventh crown wasn't broken.”

“I saw the fracture.”

“So did I. But I remember something else. When the crown spoke, I saw the hall before the burial. The six were arguing. They wanted something from the seventh. They were afraid of him, but they needed him.”

“What did they want?”

“There was a door beneath a mountain. Voices were coming from behind it. The six had opened it. The seventh was trying to close it.”

The traveler stared at the charcoal drawing. The fracture looked different now: not defeat, but sacrifice.

“Why erase him from history?”

“Because if the world remembered what he did, it would remember what they had done first.”

The hearth flared blue. Within the fire appeared a mountain with a hollow summit, a city beneath it, and a road descending into darkness.

*The first oath was spoken beneath the crown. The first oath must be remembered.*

The traveler reached for their cloak.

“We're going north.”

“To Hollow Crown? That's where the prison is!”

“That's where the answers are.”

“If the six kings were afraid of what they opened, what chance do we have?”

“None, if we stay here waiting for it to find us.”

Outside, the bells began ringing again—not from the tower, but from beneath the streets. The traveler understood that they were not warning the living. They were calling the dead.

---

Captain Edric Voss had buried thirty-seven soldiers in his years commanding the watch at Thornhaven. He remembered every name. Whenever a soldier died beneath his command, Edric wrote their name on parchment and placed it in a wooden chest beneath his bed.

“A kingdom that forgets its dead,” he had once said, “will eventually forget what it was fighting for.”

At the eastern spire, twelve soldiers waited behind him. The ancient monument was alive with silver symbols.

“Should we send for the scholars?” asked Garran.

“The scholars have spent three hundred years arguing about these stones. I suspect the stones are tired of waiting.”

Edric approached. A question entered his mind: *What is the duty of a guardian?*

“To defend those who cannot defend themselves.”

The symbols darkened. The question returned.

He remembered a winter patrol, a nobleman's carriage trapped in snow, a village burning two miles away. Ordered to escort the nobleman, Edric obeyed. By the time another patrol reached the village, seventeen people were dead, including a boy who had once brought him flowers.

“A guardian,” he answered, “must defend the innocent even when those who command him order otherwise.”

Blue light erupted. Edric fell to one knee as the countryside unfolded in his mind. Ancient roads, bridges, and towers emerged from forgotten places. Far north, beneath Hollow Crown, stood a city. Seven roads approached it. Six ended at gates; the seventh descended beneath the earth. At its center lay a crown divided in two.

When the light faded, Garran caught Edric's arm.

“What happened?”

“I saw a city that shouldn't exist. Prepare horses, supplies, and every surviving map of the northern mountains.”

“Where are we going?”

“To find out what our ancestors were so desperate to hide.”

---

Beneath the chapel, Aldric and Seraphine descended the stairway. Its walls became polished black marble carved with seven crowned figures around a circular opening. Six figures had their faces destroyed. The seventh remained untouched: young, sorrowful, and standing apart.

“Someone wanted the six forgotten,” Seraphine said.

“Or wanted them spared the shame of being remembered.”

At the bottom stood a black iron door bearing seven handprints. Six were outlined in gold. The seventh was silver. Aldric pressed his father's divided-crown ring to the silver handprint. The door opened.

Inside, seven stone thrones circled a chamber. Six were empty. Upon the seventh rested a tarnished silver crown, whole despite the deep fracture across its front.

“Reports reached the archives,” Seraphine said. “A broken crown was found beneath Ravenshollow. Then the bells began ringing.”

“Why wasn't I told?”

“The reports were dismissed as tavern stories. Now I believe every story we dismissed deserves another hearing.”

Words lit the floor.

*Six kings sought dominion over death.*

*Six kings opened the forbidden gate.*

*Six kings heard the promise of eternity.*

*One king refused.*

*One king chose the living over the immortal.*

*One king surrendered his crown.*

*One king remained when the others fled.*

Beneath them appeared a final inscription: *He was not forgotten by accident. He was forgotten by command.*

Aldric touched the crown. A vision took him into a hall beneath the mountain. Six kings stood before an enormous gate. Something vast moved behind it.

“We cannot close it!” one shouted.

“Then we must hold it!”

“With what?”

The seventh king stepped forward. “With me.”

“You will die,” whispered another.

“Perhaps. I will bind the passage. I will remain between the darkness and the world.”

“And if the binding fails?”

“Then those who inherit your crowns must remember what you did.”

One of the six shook his head. “They cannot know. The kingdoms would tear our thrones apart.”

“Then let them.”

The seventh king turned toward the gate. Behind him, the six drew their swords.

The vision shattered. Aldric fell to his knees.

“The seventh wasn't their prisoner,” he told Seraphine.

“Then what was he?”

“Their savior. They let him bind the darkness, then erased him so no one would learn why the gate had been opened.”

From the seventh throne came a faint heartbeat.

Seraphine rose. “We should leave. I don't think this chamber was built to preserve the truth. I think it was built to keep someone from finding it.”

---

Far beneath Hollow Crown, the ancient gate shuddered. Chains thicker than oak trunks crossed its surface. Each bore the name of a kingdom. Six glowed faintly. The seventh remained dark.

Beyond the gate, something stirred. It had waited through the rise and fall of empires, listened as descendants of the six kings built palaces upon stolen peace, and watched generations live and die without knowing the price paid for their existence.

Now the names were returning.

A crack appeared. Through it came a whisper.

“Their fathers opened the way. Their children shall finish what they began.”

Far above, the mountain trembled. Snow broke from its northern slopes. Ancient tunnels collapsed. Across the Seven Great Kingdoms, the Spires of Remembrance burned brighter.

In Ravenshollow, the traveler looked toward the mountains. In Thornhaven, Edric ordered horses prepared. Beneath the chapel, Aldric lifted the seventh king's crown.

Between the living world and the darkness beyond the gate, the forgotten king's voice spoke:

“Do not mistake the opening of the prison for the breaking of its chains. Find the oath. Remember my name.”

Silence fell. For the first time in a thousand years, the seventh chain began to glow.

**End of Chapter Six — The Debt of Seven Kings.**
